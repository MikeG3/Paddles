Chapter 1

Planning the Expedition

I wanted to reach Baie Fine.

At the time, I knew very little about the region. I had been introduced to the nearby Killarney area and got a glimpse of the La Cloche Mountains in the distance, but I really wanted to get a closer look.

I had heard Baie Fine mentioned briefly in a YouTube video by renowned Canadian kayaker Ken Whiting, or PaddleTV and Ted East, of Killarney Outfitters. The way he described it made it sound like somewhere worth working for. That was enough.

I wanted to return to Northern Ontario and was ready for another ambitious expedition into Crown Land. And, perhaps most importantly, I wanted to see somewhere that I assumed most people never bothered to reach.

So I opened Google Maps and started planning

I studied satellite imagery, searched for possible launch points, traced distances and prepared some back up plans. On paper, it looked ambitious, but manageable.

I would launch from a marina north of Manitoulin Island and work my way across McGregor Bay, using three small island chains to provide some shelter before committing to approximately a kilometre and a half crossing of open water. From there, I had identified what appeared to be a possible short portage into Baie Fine.

After accessing Baie Fine, I would continue toward a place known as The Pool, then make a short hike toward Lake Topaz.

I estimated that reaching the lake would take at least two days. From everything I had heard, the journey would be worth it.

The route was ambitious. The plan was optimistic. And I was excited.

On July 28, 2022, I drove roughly 750 kilometres along the Trans-Canada Highway toward Manitoulin Island, eventually stopping at a motel near the junction with Highway 6.

Chapter 2

Into the Unknown

July 29, 2022

The following morning, I left the motel and continued south toward McGregor Bay. After nearly 750 kilometres of driving, the landscape around Manitoulin Island already felt a long way removed from home.

I eventually arrived at McGregor Bay Landing, at J&G Marina, with a fully loaded sea kayak and considerably more enthusiasm than experience.

Before launching, I paid the parking fee and stopped to ask the marina employee about the area.

Naturally, one of my first questions was about bears.

My second was about Baie Fine.

I told him where I was going and explained the route I had planned. His reaction was not quite the enthusiastic encouragement I had imagined.

He wished me a bon voyage, though, and confirmed something I was hoping to hear.

Baie Fine really was worth seeing.

He also confirmed that the La Cloche Mountains were every bit as spectacular as they appeared from a distance.

That was enough to get me excited again.

I asked about the boating conditions.

At the marina, everything looked almost deceptively peaceful. The water was calm, the wind was light, and McGregor Bay appeared to be exactly the kind of place I had imagined paddling through.

Then came the warning.

"Once you get past the bay, the water opens up."

He explained that conditions could be very different once I left the protection of the marina and its surrounding islands.

I listened.

I understood the words.

I did not yet understand what they meant.

At that point in my paddling experience, I still wasn't routinely checking wind speed and direction, calculating exposure or thinking carefully about fetch before committing to an open-water route. I knew enough to be cautious, but not enough to fully understand what I should have been cautious about.

The water in front of me was calm.

My kayak was ready.

The route was planned.

Somewhere beyond those sheltered waters were the islands, the mountains, Baie Fine, The Pool and Lake Topaz.

I had spent weeks planning how to get there.

Now there was only one thing left to do.

I launched.
